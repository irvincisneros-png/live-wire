Reviewed [the spec](</Users/irvincisneros/Documents/Local Projects/Science Simulator Website/live-wire/docs/specs/2026-11-15-live-wire-design.md>). No files edited. Ordered by severity; conditional risks are identified as such.

1. **Critical — Job 2 teaches a mains-installation procedure despite its disclaimer** (L115–120). Students select cable, terminate conductors and restore supply; the realistic apprentice framing encourages transfer. **Fix:** replace it with a 12 V training-board task; retain household parallel circuits as a conceptual diagram. NSW explicitly describes DIY electrical work as dangerous and illegal. [NSW guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/electrical-safety-home)

2. **Critical — The proposed safety sequence is incomplete** (L120). Selecting a breaker and “test for dead” omits secured isolation, tester verification, alternative supplies and competent supervision. Solar/battery equipment makes the omission especially misleading. **Fix:** remove student-operated mains work; have a qualified reviewer approve any narrated professional demonstration. [SafeWork NSW](https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/hazards-a-z/electrical-and-power/electrical-work)

3. **High — Cable sizing from `I=P/V` teaches a dangerous rule** (L120,171). Load current alone cannot determine cable size: installation method, insulation, grouping, length, voltage drop and protection matter. **Fix:** remove cable-selection scoring; label listed sizes as contextual examples, never selection guidance.

4. **High — Fault-finding rewards unsafe shortcuts** (L119–120). “Fewer measurements = better” penalises verification; “you would have been shocked” incorrectly makes contact alone a deterministic shock model. **Fix:** use low-voltage faults, score justified diagnostic choices, and describe unsafe actions without pretending to predict injury.

5. **High — Protection devices are conflated** (L83,98,108,121,132). A short is not simply current above a limit; an overload breaker, power-board protector and RCD have different functions. Two sockets may share one circuit. **Fix:** model and label protection separately; give Job 3 explicit circuit membership and board/circuit limits, with no implication that protection makes contact safe.

6. **High — The glow model confuses voltage with energy attached to an electron** (L51,58). Voltage is potential difference, in J/C. The inverse-potential glow can illustrate electron potential energy relative to a reference, but cannot be called voltage everywhere. **Fix:** label it “energy-transfer analogy”; teach `ΔE=QΔV` and show voltage between two labelled points.

7. **High — “Dots always move negative → positive” cannot hold everywhere** (L19,57,62). It describes external metallic conductors in a simple discharging DC circuit; it does not describe AC mains or charge transport inside every source. **Fix:** constrain electron dots to metallic DC bench circuits; use a separately labelled energy-flow display for the house.

8. **High — Overnight scope has no credible minimum release** (L75–89,113–134,186–189). Eight guided stations, five assessments, several bespoke scenes and full verification create a large integration tail. **Fix:** define an overnight release of the solver, accessible bench and one complete station; gate other modules behind passing integration checks.

9. **High — Parallel-build contracts are names, not contracts** (L159–162). Missing: part schemas, terminal ordering, units, sign conventions, IDs, mutation rules, events, errors and concrete host APIs. `JobDef.score()` also has no defined relationship to its mounted instance. **Fix:** freeze complete types, fixtures and a working reference implementation before delegation.

10. **High — Singular solver failures become plausible misinformation** (L107). Returning “no current” hides inconsistent circuits, numerical failures and implementation bugs. **Fix:** return explicit `solved | floating | inconsistent | invalid` statuses; suppress authoritative readouts and scoring when unsolved.

11. **High — Independently referenced islands produce meaningless cross-island voltages** (L93,105–107). Connecting the finite-resistance voltmeter can also change the topology. **Fix:** include meter leads in the solve; mark undefined floating measurements; never subtract arbitrary island references.

12. **High — No shared simulation clock or state-transition contract** (L59,98,123,161). Energy, trips, warm-up and battery state can diverge with frame rate, background tabs or route changes. **Fix:** define deterministic stepping, pause/resume, time acceleration, reset behaviour and event order; render solved state without advancing physics.

13. **High — AC appliance equations are overgeneralised** (L71,87,120–121). At 230 V RMS, `P=VI` and `I=P/V` apply directly to the intended resistive approximation, not every appliance. **Fix:** restrict assessed loads to resistive examples and state unity power factor; otherwise use specified current/power-factor data. [AC power reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/15-4-power-in-an-ac-circuit)

14. **High — Ohm’s law is presented as universal** (L71,86,106). A fixed-resistance “filament” with changing colour and temperature reinforces that real lamps have straight-line V–I behaviour. **Fix:** teach the relationship with a fixed-temperature resistor; label the lamp approximation persistently; show `V=IR` only for appropriate components. Specify graph axes: gradient is R only for V vertically against I horizontally. [Ohm’s law](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-4-ohms-law)

15. **High — Dot counting does not yet represent amperes** (L49–50,57,82). No charge per dot is defined, and quality tiers reduce dot counts. Students could obtain different “currents” on different devices. **Fix:** define representative charge and branch flux conservation; keep numerical current independent of rendering density and explain `I=ΔQ/Δt`.

16. **High — Solar sizing is underspecified enough to reward impossible systems** (L123). Missing: timestep, PV profile, usable capacity, charge/discharge power, losses, initial/final state of charge, imports, exports and unmet demand. **Fix:** publish the complete energy-balance contract and fixtures; prevent free initial battery energy and distinguish kW from kWh.

17. **High — Accessibility stops at a partial keyboard scheme** (L45,97,133). Canvas objects, flying leads, graphs, coach steps and house tasks lack equivalent controls and readable state. **Fix:** provide semantic DOM controls and a circuit table/diagram supporting every assessed action; specify focus order, announcements, contrast, zoom and complete keyboard operation. [W3C interaction requirements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)

18. **High — Chromebook performance has no acceptance budget** (L129–134). Transparent wires, glass, bloom, shadows, particles and post-processing can remain expensive even with fewer dots. A startup probe misses sustained throttling. **Fix:** name baseline devices and budgets for sustained frame time, memory, draw calls and loading; provide a functional 2D fallback and WebGL-context-loss recovery.

19. **High — Resource ownership is undefined** (L144–163). “Fully dispose” can make one station destroy shared materials while another leaks render loops, listeners or GPU targets. **Fix:** assign ownership of renderer, animation loop, controls, shared assets and subscriptions; test repeated mount/unmount cycles.

20. **High — Parallel integration and deployment are uncontrolled** (L138,186–189). No file ownership, dependency freeze, integration owner or merge gate exists; every main push deploys. **Fix:** assign module ownership, prohibit unilateral shared-contract changes, integrate through checked branches and require a final release gate.

21. **Medium — Resistance is misleadingly pictured as denser atoms** (L52,67). Resistance also depends on material, length, area and temperature; drift speed depends on carrier density and area, not current alone. “Well under a millimetre per second” is not universal. **Fix:** show scattering without density-as-resistance; qualify the speed claim and use `R=ρL/A` in explanatory material. [Current and resistivity](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-summary)

22. **Medium — Energy accounting lacks boundaries and signs** (L59,71,88). Sources may have negative absorbed power; stored thermal energy means instantaneous input need not equal outgoing heat/light. **Fix:** define passive sign convention, source delivery, tally reset and `input = output + change in storage`; use `1 kWh = 3.6 MJ`.

23. **Medium — Lamp efficiency and brightness comparisons lack a controlled service** (L88,98). Comparing different brightnesses can teach “uses fewer watts” as equivalent to “more efficient”; lumens/W is not a percentage energy efficiency. **Fix:** compare equal light output, define useful output explicitly, and label proportional brightness, 150% failure and 1.5-second trips as game approximations.

24. **Medium — Meter and fault semantics are missing** (L96–108,119). Ammeter misconnection can short the supply; “loose connection” could mean open, resistive or intermittent. **Fix:** define meter modes, placement and protection, plus one precise fault model per seed; assess meter connection on the low-voltage bench first.

25. **Medium — Australian wiring facts mix examples with general rules** (L171). Red/black remains a valid fixed-wiring example; brown/blue is not simply a chronological “new cable” replacement. A 10 A socket rating is not the circuit rating. **Fix:** distinguish fixed wiring from flexible cords, label the simulated installation’s convention, and state that colour never establishes isolation. [Energy Safe Victoria colour guidance](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Guidelines_Colour_vision_deficiency_Jun2020.pdf)

26. **Medium — Energy Rating treatment is partly correct but overclaims running cost** (L87,174). Same-type/similar-size comparison is appropriate; label consumption reflects standardised usage, not guaranteed household consumption. Not every product label is simply annual kWh. **Fix:** use an authentic supported appliance label, explain its test assumptions and distinguish label estimates from actual-use calculations. [Official label guidance](https://www.energyrating.gov.au/consumer-information/understand-energy-rating-label)

27. **Medium — Generation comparisons lack comparable definitions** (L89,173). Agents could conflate efficiency with capacity factor, use incompatible cost boundaries, or show every thermal plant boiling water. **Fix:** specify each transformation chain and dated comparison dataset; distinguish gas turbines, steam cycles and direct PV conversion; identify omitted sources as omissions. [Generation mechanisms](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/how-electricity-is-generated.php)

28. **Medium — Several claimed assessments do not test the stated learning** (L35–39,84,120–121). Watching an automatic diagram is not drawing one; cable/load calculations are not an Ohm’s-law investigation. **Fix:** add independent diagram construction and measured V–I investigation evidence; revise the coverage map to match what students actually demonstrate.

29. **Medium — Scoring and “best possible” are undefined** (L119–125). Missing: rubrics, tolerances, retry rules, seed equivalence, baseline assumptions and upgrade interactions. Revealing live values or X-ray state may also reveal the hidden fault. **Fix:** publish scoring fixtures and visibility rules; separate practice from submitted attempts and constrain optimisation to an explicit finite dataset.

30. **Medium — Persistence and evidence cannot reliably support summative claims** (L125,165,167). Shared-browser names can persist; results lack specified content version, seed and attempt ID; debug hooks can alter state. **Fix:** provide clear/reset/export controls and versioned evidence metadata; describe results as teacher-reviewed formative evidence unless stronger assessment controls are added.

31. **Medium — Release criteria are largely subjective or incomplete** (L12,109,134,175–182). Screenshots cannot establish operability; `1e-6` lacks units and absolute/relative meaning. Pages base paths, the teacher HTML entry and offline behaviour are unspecified. **Fix:** add browser interaction tests, conservation/sign/meter tests, frame-rate invariance and defined tolerances; verify deployed subpath routing, teacher-page output, print layout and storage-disabled operation. Replace “better than PhET” with measurable learning and usability criteria.
